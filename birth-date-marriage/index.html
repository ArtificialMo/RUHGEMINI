Do birth dates show historical patterns in marriage?

This project groups historical records by recurring calendar birth-date ranges traditionally labeled Taurus, Cancer, Leo and so on. It asks whether measurable pairing or relationship patterns appear in the data.
Scientific framing: this dashboard does not assume astrology predicts the future. Zodiac names are used only as familiar labels for birth-date ranges. Any association found would not establish a celestial, supernatural or religious cause.

What this means

With millions of records, very small deviations can be statistically detectable while still being practically tiny. A +0.6% marriage-frequency excess is not a 60% compatibility score and says nothing by itself about divorce or happiness.

Important: birth season, age matching, data-entry artifacts and multiple testing can all create apparent patterns. Large datasets must be cleaned and compared with the correct baseline.

Divorce evidence

Do specific date-group pairs divorce more?

Strongest direct test: Sweden

A longitudinal Swedish register study analyzed 66,063 unions and tested traditional astrological compatibility classifications against partner selection and divorce. The compatibility measures did not show a robust divorce advantage; a continuous compatibility score produced a hazard ratio around 1.00.

Selected findings

What the evidence actually supports.

Taurus × Cancer

About +0.62% pooled above expectation in the cleaned England & Wales marriage matrix: a small marriage-frequency excess, not evidence of destiny or marital success.

Leo × Pisces

About −0.02% from expectation in the same matrix — essentially indistinguishable from random pairing at this scale.

Belief effects

China, Vietnam and Japan provide evidence that culturally held birth-date or birth-year beliefs can alter real choices. That is a social-behavior effect, not proof of celestial prediction.
