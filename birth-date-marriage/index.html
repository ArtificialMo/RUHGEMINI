Marriage ≠ divorce

What do outcome data say?

Sweden · strongest direct longitudinal test

66,063 unions

Helgertz & Scott tested several published compatibility classifications against both partner selection and divorce. Results failed to show a consistent advantage for supposedly compatible couples. A continuous compatibility measure was effectively null for divorce risk.

Michigan · older direct test

2,978 marriages + 478 divorces

Silverman compared explicit “compatible” and “incompatible” pair lists from two astrologers with Michigan marriage/divorce records. The marriage and divorce distributions did not support those predictions.

Public-data limitation

No credible 78-pair divorce matrix

We did not find a public research-grade Western dataset that publishes a trustworthy divorce percentage for every one of the 78 unique pairings. The site therefore does not invent pair-specific divorce rates.

Cross-country interpretation

Where the data are genuinely interesting.

England & Wales

The cleaned 9.5M-couple matrix is remarkably close to random. The largest of 144 oriented deviations in Voas’s paper is barely 1%.

France

A 6.50M-marriage secondary analysis found a significant raw chi-square pattern, but exact-same-birthday marriages were about 3.4× the simple expectation, showing how administrative/copying artifacts can create false structure.

China & Vietnam

Traditional compatibility beliefs can alter who actually marries whom and, in China, who remains single. That is strong evidence for a social belief effect, not celestial prediction.

Japan

The Firehorse superstition has produced measurable demographic behavior. Again, culturally held beliefs can change real outcomes even when the proposed supernatural mechanism is not established.

Method note. The interactive marriage counts use the cleaned England & Wales matrix reported by David Voas from ONS 2001 Census commissioned tables.
